## SHORT 1 — „Never Drink the Ocean” (din Part 1, Ch. 2)
Titlu: Why Castaways Can't Drink Seawater · Hashtags: #survival #ocean #shorts

[NARRATION]
He was dying of thirst… surrounded by water.
Teo had been adrift for a day. Lips cracked. Sun burning. And all around him — cool, endless ocean.
He cupped his hands.
Mara grabbed his wrist. "Don't."
Here's why. Seawater is about three and a half percent salt. Your kidneys can only make urine that's less salty than that.
So to flush out the salt you just drank, your body spends more water than you took in.
Every sip makes you thirstier. Drink enough, and the ocean dries you out from the inside.
Teo let the water fall through his fingers.
And that's the cruelest joke of being lost at sea:
he was dying of thirst… surrounded by water.

## SHORT 2 — „How a Tarp Saved Their Lives” (din Part 1, Ch. 3)
Titlu: The Trick That Kept Castaways Alive for 133 Days · Hashtags: #survival #raft #shorts

[NARRATION]
In 1942, a sailor survived one hundred and thirty-three days alone on a raft.
His name was Poon Lim. And one of the things that kept him alive was simple: he caught the rain.
Two castaways, Mara and Teo, had three empty bottles and one plastic tarp.
When the storm clouds came, Mara moved fast. Tie two corners down. Push the middle into a bowl. Hold the edges.
Then the sky opened.
In ten minutes, three bottles were full.
Poon Lim used the canvas cover of his life jacket the same way.
No filter. No machine. Just a sheet and a sky.
Because out there, every cloud is a water tank.
And it was how one man survived one hundred and thirty-three days.

## SHORT 3 — „Something Hit the Raft” (din Part 1, Ch. 7 + Part 2, Ch. 1)
Titlu: Something Huge Bumped Their Raft From Below · Hashtags: #ocean #sealife #shorts

[NARRATION]
Something hit the raft… from below.
The ocean was flat. No wind. No waves.
Then — a bump. Solid. Heavy.
Mara and Teo stared into the water. Nothing.
Then a shadow slid beneath them. Longer than the raft. Much longer.
It turned around… and came back.
Teo grabbed the knife. Mara stopped him. "Look at its mouth."
No teeth. Just a huge, open filter.
A whale shark — the biggest fish in the ocean, up to around twelve meters long. And harmless. It eats plankton.
It circled them once, brushed the wood… and sank back into the blue.
"I think," Teo whispered, "it was just saying hello."
Not every monster under the raft is a monster.
But they didn't know that when something hit the raft… from below.

## SHORT 4 — „Fresh Water From the Sun” (din Part 2, Ch. 3)
Titlu: They Turned Seawater Into Drinking Water · Hashtags: #survival #science #shorts

[NARRATION]
One drop. That's all they needed to see.
No rain for days. Mara and Teo were running out of time.
So they built a machine — from trash.
A cut-open bottle. A square of clear plastic. A little seawater in a dark container.
The sun heats the water. Water evaporates. The salt stays behind.
The vapor hits the cool plastic, turns back into drops… and runs down into the bottle.
Fresh water. From the ocean. Powered by the sun.
It's called a solar still. Steven Callahan used solar stills during his seventy-six days adrift in the Atlantic in 1982 — they were slow and stubborn, and they produced only a little each day.
But a little every day is the difference between life and death.
And it all starts with one drop.

## SHORT 5 — „The Ship That Didn't Stop” (din Part 2, Ch. 6)
Titlu: The Ship Sailed Right Past Them · Hashtags: #lostatsea #survival #shorts

[NARRATION]
After thirty-one days at sea… a ship.
A giant cargo ship, two or three miles away.
Mara and Teo screamed. Waved the tarp. Teo climbed the roof post and swung his shirt.
Mara flashed sunlight toward the bridge with a scrap of plastic.
The ship didn't slow down. It didn't turn.
It just… kept going.
From the bridge of a ship that size, a small raft is a speck in the waves and glare. Easy to miss.
It happened in real life too. Steven Callahan watched several ships pass him while he was adrift — most accounts say nine. None of them stopped.
Teo sat down and stared at the empty horizon.
Because the worst thing that can happen after thirty-one days at sea… is a ship.

## SHORT 6 — „How to Find Yourself With One Star” (din Part 2, Ch. 5)
Titlu: How Sailors Found Their Position With One Star · Hashtags: #navigation #stars #shorts

[NARRATION]
Lost in the middle of the ocean — and one star tells you where you are.
Mara pointed up. Not at the brightest star. At the one that never moves.
Polaris. The North Star.
Here's the trick sailors have used for centuries: in the northern hemisphere, the height of the North Star above the horizon is roughly equal to your latitude.
Star on the horizon? You're near the equator.
Star straight overhead? You're at the North Pole.
Mara held her fist at arm's length, stacking it from the sea up to the star.
It's not precise. Off by a few degrees, maybe more.
But after twenty-six days of knowing nothing… it was something.
Because when you're lost in the middle of the ocean, one star tells you where you are.
